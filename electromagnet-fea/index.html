Electromagnet Upgrade: Checking Plate Saturation with Magnetostatic FEA

Before moving Vivvity's clamp from four small electromagnets to two 160 lbf magnets, I checked whether the steel plate could actually carry the flux.

Result

FEA supported replacing four 26 lbf cylindrical magnets with two 160 lbf rectangular magnets. After physical testing, I switched the clamp over, and it now holds a reliable chip seal to 30 psi.

Why it needed checking: plate saturation

The June gantry and lock test used four cylindrical electromagnets rated at 26 lbf each, pulling against a 1018 steel plate on the electrode block. I wanted more clamping margin and a simpler layout.

All of the clamping force passes through that plate. It is thin because Vivvity's vertical travel is tightly constrained, and a thin plate can only carry so much flux before it saturates. Past that point, more coil current adds very little force, and the extra flux leaks into the surroundings instead of pulling on the plate.

My hand calculations put the plate's flux density at about 2.3 T with the new magnets, which is above where 1018 steel saturates. That was enough of a warning that I didn't want to buy and install the magnets without checking properly.

Impact loading was not a concern. The gantry lowers the plate into contact before the magnets are energized, so they never snap the plate in from a distance.

Simulation setup

I built an Ansys Mechanical magnetostatic model of the plate and both magnets:

  • Materials: a nonlinear B-H curve for 1018 steel, so the model could show saturation instead of assuming linear behavior
  • Coils: modeled as stranded conductors with the magnets' current and turn count
  • Air domain: an enclosure around the full assembly so the model could capture flux leakage
  • Shared topology between touching bodies, which the magnetostatic solver needs to treat them as connected

To get the clamping force, I summed force over the whole plate body rather than reading the flux contour plot, because contour maximums are local mesh extremes, not the total force on the part. I refined the model setup until both magnets gave consistent results.
