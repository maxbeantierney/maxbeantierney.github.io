Gantry and Electromagnetic Lock Test

The first test of electromagnet lock and release, controlled from firmware, plus crash-prevention logic for the Vivvity gantry.

Result

  • The gantry seats 1 mm before locking and lifts 1 mm on release, so residual magnetism can't disturb later moves
  • Any move at or below chip height first goes to a safe height
  • Independent optical limit switches on each motor keep the dual-motor axes square after homing

Problem

The electrode block has to lock onto the chip reliably, release cleanly, and never be driven into the chip by a bad command or a GUI mistake.

What I did

Lock sequence. I added electromagnet control to the firmware and GUI. During locking, the gantry moves the electrodes down 1 mm to seat the magnets against the steel plate. On release it moves up 1 mm. In the video you can hear a click as the four electromagnets lock onto the plate.

Crash prevention. Every GUI button calls a macro defined in firmware, so the safety logic lives at the lowest level. Any move at or below chip height automatically goes to a safe height first.

Square homing. The Y and Z axes each use two motors in an open-loop design, with an independent optical limit switch per motor, so the gantry homes perfectly straight.

What came next

This test used four 26 lbf magnets. I later moved to two 160 lbf magnets after FEA and integrated the syringe pump for a full closed-loop pressure run.
