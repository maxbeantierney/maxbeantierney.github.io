Flagship case study

Vivvity: Automated Microfluidic Processing Instrument

An automated instrument for DNA/RNA analysis on microfluidic chips, built in the Tripathi Lab at Brown and developed with Revvity as the successor to the LabChip GxII Touch.

Role: Lead Engineer, Gantry & Control SystemsTripathi Lab, Brown UniversityMay 2025 – present

Result

  • Complete cycle demonstrated (August 2026): home, lower onto the chip, clamp, pressurize, release, and park. Watch the run.
  • 30 psi chip seal from the electromagnetic clamp, held for 90+ seconds in testing.
  • Pressure held to about 0.15 psi of setpoint by a closed-loop syringe-pump controller.
  • Two-magnet clamp adopted after magnetostatic FEA and bench testing. See the analysis.

My role

I led the mechanical and electromechanical integration of the gantry, electromagnetic clamp, pressure-control hardware, firmware, and operator interface. The electrode block itself is supplied by Revvity.

The problem

Chip-based nucleic acid assays need precise, repeatable fluid handling under controlled pressure. When people load, clamp, and pressurize chips by hand, the results vary, throughput is limited, and the process is hard to scale. Vivvity automates those steps in a compact, programmable platform. It also has to fit inside a larger liquid handler, so its vertical travel is tightly limited.

System architecture

  1. Motion controlDual lead-screw gantry on horizontal and vertical axes. BTT Octopus v1.1 controller, with independent optical limit switches for each motor.
  2. Electromagnetic clampTwo 160 lbf rectangular electromagnets pulling a 1018 steel plate on the electrode block.
  3. Pressure sensing & controlSyringe pump on its own stepper axis. Honeywell MPRLS sensor over custom I²C, and a three-phase control loop.
  4. Electrode interfaceRevvity-supplied electrode block with a 5-pin high-voltage input to the chip.
  5. Operator GUIA Python interface replacing Pronterface: homing, pressurization, and a cleaning mode.

Every GUI button calls a macro defined in firmware, so safety logic lives at the lowest level of the system.

What I built

Mechanism

The dual lead-screw motion system works within tight vertical limits so it doesn't hit the main liquid handler's pipette toolhead. Lead screws are self-locking, so the mechanism holds the clamp load without the motors drawing current. The electrode block has to land flat and in the same place every cycle, because even a small deviation breaks the seal.

Sensing

Pressure is read from a Honeywell MPRLS sensor through a custom I²C interface I wrote into the Marlin firmware. The firmware tares it at the start of each session, so readings are referenced to atmosphere.

Firmware & control

I added custom G-code commands for work outside normal motion: electromagnet lock and release, pressurization sequences, and a three-phase pressure controller (fast feedforward, fine approach, then proportional hold with anti-stiction dither). Crash-prevention logic moves the gantry to a safe height before any move at or below chip level.

Operator interface

I built a Python GUI that lab staff with no technical background can run reliably. It shows live telemetry and has one-button homing, pressurization, and cleaning.

Test & validation

I checked clamp plate saturation with hand calculations and Ansys magnetostatic FEA, then confirmed the seal on a bench rig. I also isolated the syringe pump and sensor to tune the control loop before full-system runs.

Status

The gantry, clamp, and pressure subsystems now run together through a full automated cycle. Work continues on integrating them into the larger instrument.
