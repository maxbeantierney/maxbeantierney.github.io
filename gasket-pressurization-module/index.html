Novel Gasket Design for an Automated Pressurization Module

A Flexible 80A SLA gasket printed on a Formlabs Form 4, with five times the contact area of a standard rim seal.

Independent project, Tripathi LabFebruary 2026

Result

  • 5× greater contact area than a standard rim seal, for a more reliable, consistent seal across repeated cycles
  • Presented at SLAS2026, the Society for Laboratory Automation and Screening International Conference and Exhibition (February 7–11, 2026, Boston)

Problem

Priming a microfluidic chip means pushing gel and buffer into its channels under pressure before a run. If the seal between the priming hardware and the chip's wells leaks even slightly, the chip primes inconsistently, and that variability carries through to the assay results.

Design

The chip-priming module was designed around closed-loop pressure sensing, with a separate sensor in the line to verify the pressure delivered to the chip. My main contribution was the gasket, the part that decides whether any of that pressure actually reaches the chip.

A standard seal presses down on the rim of each sample well. My gasket seals around the sides of the well instead. It mounts so that the lip of a counterbore sits flush with each well's rim, which puts it in contact with both the rim and the outside wall of the well.

Fabrication

I printed the gasket on a Formlabs Form 4 SLA printer in Flexible 80A resin. The flexible resin let the counterbore conform to each well while still holding the fine geometry needed to line up with the chip's well pattern. I fabricated and tested the gasket itself, since the rest of the module depended on it. The closed-loop sensing module was designed but not built.

Post-processing details

20-minute wash in isopropyl alcohol, a rinse in water, then a 10-minute UV cure at 35 °C.
